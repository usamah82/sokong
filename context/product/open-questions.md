# Open questions

Answer these in this file, or in a reply that an agent then records here. Do not let an agent pick an answer in order to unblock a spec.

The questions that change the first PRD are Q1, Q2, Q4, Q6, and Q8. Q1 changes whether the product is a caseload or a census. Q2 and Q6 change who the first user is and what a boundary means. Q4 changes the meaning of every status on screen. Q8 changes what an on-behalf enrolment is allowed to store.

| ID | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Is Mara's percentage computed from every family in a locality, or only from families inside Sokong? | The accountability slides need a share of thriving families. The database slides only store struggling and deteriorating families. A census and a caseload are different products. |
| Q2 | Who operates the first version, and in which place? | A residents' association, a representative's office, an NGO, and a ministry imply different accounts, custody of the data, and audit. The Malay names and the last slide suggest Malaysia. The framework is written as universal. |
| Q3 | Does the app stop at record and coordination, while the help itself is given outside it? | The deck's treatments are counseling, housing, food, jobs, and scholarships. The app may only record that they happened and connect the people. |
| Q4 | Which rule sets the status: the generation-by-wealth table, or the four ordered boxes in Sarah's story? | The table says struggling means an independent generation and minimal wealth. The story calls a family struggling once it is a good family unit with basic needs met, even when the children are not yet independent. |
| Q5 | Who may mark "good family unit," from what evidence, and can the family see and contest it? | The source warns that grading inner character discriminates. The story still has a worker uncheck the box. |
| Q6 | What is a locality in the first version? | A housing block, a village, a district, and a constituency produce different directories and different planner views. |
| Q7 | What is the closed list of basic needs? | Roof and food are named. The story adds rent, utilities, and disability care. |
| Q8 | What may be stored when a teacher enrols a child's family, before a parent consents at the visit? | Self-enrolment is said to record consent. On-behalf enrolment happens first, and the visit collects consent after. |
| Q9 | Is there one recipient list, or a separate list for each organization that refuses to pool? | The program wants one database. A loose end says NGOs will not share. |
| Q10 | In the first version, are external events typed by a worker, or received from school, hospital, police, court, tax, and social security? | The track activity names those agencies. Nothing says they are connected. |
| Q11 | Are people outside a family unit in the first version? | The loose ends name runaways, orphans, people dependent on drugs, and people without a home. |
| Q12 | When the families who qualify outnumber the help, who sets the order? | The source asks the question and does not answer it. |
| Q13 | Are benefactors and facilitators verified, the way recipient families are? | The loose ends include scam charities. The benefactor's complaint includes fake charities. |
| Q14 | Are prevention, and a campaign or curriculum for the three values, part of this app? | Both are named as missing work. Both can be a movement rather than a feature. |
| Q15 | Which languages do the first web and mobile clients speak? | The source document is in English. The product names are Malay. |
| Q16 | Do the undefined labels Good, Self-sufficient, and Broken, applied to people, become part of the model? | They appear only on the loose-end slide. The body defines good and broken family units, not three kinds of person. |
